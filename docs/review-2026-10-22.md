# Tourganiser — Full Review, 22 October 2026

A walkthrough of the whole application, done without reference to the existing docs first,
then cross-checked against them (section 8). Nothing in the code was changed.

## How this was done

- **Code read:** all of `api/src` and the bulk of `tourganiser-ui/src` (pages, tournament
  view, create flow, modals, request layer, auth, styles where relevant).
- **Live walkthrough:** both dev servers on localhost, against the Neon database in
  `api/.env` — which holds the real TuksVolleyball tournaments, so it is effectively
  production (see 7.4).
  - As an **anonymous visitor**: Home, Discover, the TuksVolleyball HST 2026 tournament
    (Overview, Fixtures & Schedule, Standings, Knockout, Final Rankings, Teams), at desktop
    and phone widths.
  - As an **organiser**, using a throwaway account: signup, create tournament (a 12-team
    pool+knockout division and a 5-team league), Teams, schedule maker and generator, save
    schedule, start, score entry, round progression, Settings, print view, Profile, end,
    delete.
- Timings below were measured from this PC against the remote database, so absolute
  numbers will be lower in production. The ratios and the query counts still apply.

**Severity:** **Critical** — wrong results or a security hole in production.
**High** — common flows are hard or broken. **Medium** — noticeable friction or waste.
**Low** — polish.
**Known** marks items already recorded in `docs/` (section 8).

## 2. UX — the spectator side

Most people who open a tournament link are players, parents and coaches on a phone at the
venue. The organiser screens are well built. The spectator side is where people who are not
you will struggle most.

### 2.1 Home page

- **The features section works against you.** Screenshots are washed out and partly
  illegible. Text sits on top of busy images, worst on "Custom Rules". Several images show
  UI that no longer exists ("Volleyball Type: Indoor/Beach", "Custom Tournament — Coming
  soon"). Medium.
- **Overclaims.** "Analytics — detailed insights … generate comprehensive reports" is not a
  feature. "Support for various competition styles and scoring systems" oversells two
  formats and no match-format setting. A first-time organiser who believes these will be
  disappointed. Medium.
- **Messaging says "sports", art says football/hockey, product is volleyball only.** Lead
  with volleyball, as the About page already does. Low.
- **No live content.** A strip of "Happening now" or "Upcoming" tournaments would make the
  home page useful for returning visitors. A signed-in organiser sees the same marketing
  page every time. "Your tournaments / continue setting up" belongs here. Medium.
- The donation banner sits above the features. Ask for money after showing value. Low.

### 2.2 Discover

- **Search by name only.** No filter by date, location or status, and no sort. "Completed"
  will grow forever with no paging. Medium.
- **Test tournaments are public.** "Test" has been "Ongoing" since 25 Sept, a month past its
  end date. Every tournament is public from the moment it is created. There is no
  draft/unlisted state and no prompt to finish a tournament after its end date. Medium.
- **Every card says "TOURNAMENT".** The list endpoint sends no type, so the fallback label
  always shows. Remove it or send the division formats. Low.
- **Cards are `<button>`s, not links.** `pages/Browse.jsx:347`. You can't open a tournament
  in a new tab, middle-click, or long-press to copy its link, and that is how people share.
  The saved-tournament card also nests a `<button>` inside the card `<button>`
  (`Browse.jsx:364`), which is invalid HTML and confuses screen readers. Use `<Link>`.
  Medium.

### 2.3 Tournament view — general

- **No live updates.** Verified: nothing polls. The Fixtures page is the venue scoreboard,
  and it is frozen until someone reloads. The ETag work already makes this cheap: poll
  every 15–30s while the tab is visible and it costs a 304 most of the time. Do 5.3 first
  so a 304 really is cheap. High.
- **Load time.** 2–3s locally for a 64-team tournament, before any phone-network latency.
  See section 5. Medium.
- **Shareability.**
  - The chosen division isn't in the URL, so you can't send someone "Girls U15 standings".
  - Fixture filters aren't in the URL either.
  - Every page has the same `<title>`, so browser tabs, history and bookmarks can't tell
    tournaments apart.
  - There are no Open Graph tags, so a link pasted into WhatsApp (the main way these links
    travel) shows a generic preview.
  Medium.
- **Save button never shows saved state.** Verified: save, reload, and it says "Save"
  again. The view never reads `saved_tournaments`, so there's no way to unsave from the
  tournament page. A signed-out click shows a red "You must be logged in" toast instead of
  offering sign-in. The comment in `TournamentShell.jsx:123` still says the endpoints answer
  501. Medium.
- **Back button.** Always labelled "Browse", even when you came from Profile, and it
  `replace`s the history entry, so the browser's own Back behaves oddly afterwards. Low.

### 2.4 Overview tab

- **Single-day events read "19 September 2026 — 19 September 2026".** Collapse it to one
  date. Low.
- **Finished divisions show "Round: Finals" and a progress bar.** Show the champion and
  podium instead. That is what people open a finished tournament for. Medium.
- **"Recent results" is not recent.** It is sorted by `match_no`
  (`tournamentViewFormatter.js`, `buildDivisionOverview` and the dashboard), and match
  numbers restart in each division. For HST it shows only Girls U17/U15 games, the
  highest-numbered. Sort by when the result was entered, or by scheduled time. Medium.
- The "v" in result rows sits at a different x position on each row. Low.

### 2.5 Fixtures & Schedule

- **Match numbers repeat across divisions.** At 08:00 in HST there are four different
  "#1"s. Spectators, scorekeepers and printed scoresheets all reference "#1". Prefix with a
  division code (`GU15-1`) or number matches tournament-wide in schedule order. High.
- **Status colours read backwards.** Completed = red, live = green, upcoming = blue
  (`styles/tournament-view.css:1149-1162`). In sports UIs red means LIVE, and elsewhere red
  means error. Use a red "LIVE" pill (pulsing), a neutral check or "FT" for completed, and
  the start time for upcoming. Medium.
- **No "now".** HST's single day is a 142-card list with no jump-to-now and no
  "playing now / next up" grouping. A spectator wants "what's on court 4 now, and when is my
  team on next". Medium.
- **Filters eat the phone screen.** On a 375px screen the site header, tournament header and
  five filters take ~600px before the first fixture. Collapse filters behind one "Filter"
  button. Make Team the default visible filter and remember it per tournament. Medium.
- **Winners aren't emphasised.** Both team names have the same weight, and the per-set
  scores are small and low contrast. Bold the winner and dim the loser. Low.
- **No court × time grid for the public.** It exists in the organiser's schedule maker and
  the print page. A read-only grid ("court board") would suit tablets and projectors at the
  venue. See gaps, 9.1. Low.

### 2.6 Standings, bracket, final rankings

- **No qualification markers.** Nothing shows who is through. Highlight the qualifying
  places per pool, or draw a cut line. Medium.
- **The ranking criterion is invisible.** With FIVB or simplified points there is no "Pts"
  column, so viewers can't see why a team is above another. `StandingsTab.jsx:20` still
  says "no points system exists" — out of date since 1.1. Add a Pts column when the basis is
  points, and a one-line "Ranked by …" note. Medium.
- **Finished divisions open on Pool/League.** Default to Final Rankings once a division is
  complete. Low.
- **Tied places look like real places.** Final rankings show 5, 6, 7, 8 for four
  quarterfinal losers whose order comes from a tiebreak. Show "=5", or explain it. Low.
- **The bracket names no champion.** Add a trophy or highlight on the final's winner. Low.

### 2.7 Teams tab

- **Teams aren't clickable.** The single most useful spectator page is missing: a **team
  page** with its fixtures (court and time), results, pool position and next match, and a
  shareable link. See 9.1. High.

## 3. UX — the organiser side

The create flow and schedule maker are strong. Drafts, inline validation, the review
screen and the generation report are all things most tournament tools get wrong. The issues
below are mostly about **flow between features** and **courtside speed**.

### 3.1 Create tournament

- **Structure is asked before teams.** Pools and qualifiers come at step 2, teams at step 3.
  An organiser usually knows the teams first. Ask for teams first and then *suggest* a
  structure ("12 teams → 4 pools of 3, top 8 advance" or "3 pools of 4, top 2 + 2 best
  thirds"), showing the match count each option produces. Medium.
- **The step count changes.** "Step 1 of 2" becomes "step 2 of 3" after you pick a format.
  Low.
- **The add-many-teams box is small.** It is 3 lines tall and sits next to a second
  "Cancel" button (one for the box, one for the modal). Make it taller, rename its button to
  "Close", and allow tab-separated paste from a spreadsheet. Low.
- **The review shows "Rank 1 vs Rank 8".** The live view already says "A1 vs D2". Use the
  same labels here so the organiser can sanity-check crossovers before creating. Low.
- **Creation is slow.** 10.5s for 17 teams and 30 matches (5.4). HST-sized events will take
  far longer and may hit timeouts. Medium.
- **"Sign In required" flashes.** A signed-in organiser opening `/tournaments/create`
  directly sees it briefly, because `isLoggedIn` is false until `check-login` returns
  (`CreateTournament.jsx:182`). Expose a "session checked" flag in `AuthProvider`. Low.
- **Past dates aren't allowed.** You can't record a tournament that already happened.
  Medium-low.
- **No match format.** There is nowhere to say best-of-3 or best-of-5, or 25/15-point sets
  (3.4, 9.1). Known.

### 3.2 After creation — no guided setup

- **Start Tournament is the most prominent button on a brand-new tournament.** It
  irreversibly locks teams and divisions. A first-timer is likely to press it before
  seeding, scheduling or adding editors. Replace it, while the tournament is Not Started,
  with a **setup checklist**: teams & seeding → schedule → scoresheet → editors → start.
  Each step should show done/not done. High.
- **Divisions are alphabetical** (`ORDER BY name`) with no manual ordering. Low.
- **The division delete icon sits next to Standings/Teams.** It is red, but it's still an
  easy mis-tap on a phone. Low.

### 3.3 Schedule maker

- **All toolbar buttons look the same.** Discard Changes and Reset Schedule look exactly
  like Generate and Save. Make Save primary, Generate secondary, and Reset/Discard quiet or
  destructive. Medium.
- **Enter doesn't add a court** (verified). The team field does add on Enter. Low.
- **Draft actions sound saved.** "Court 1 added to the tournament schedule" appears for an
  unsaved change. Say "added" and show an "Unsaved changes" indicator. Low.
- **Two places say how many courts.** The generator's "Courts" number duplicates the Courts
  & Fields list. Low.
- **Knockouts start right after pools.** The default leaves "break before knockout rounds"
  off. Verified: a team's last pool match was at 12:00 and its quarterfinal at 12:30, with
  30-minute matches. That leaves zero rest, and no time for the organiser to press "Start
  Next Round". Default the break on (15–30 min). Medium (rest issue Known).
- **Multi-day spread.** A 2-day test event put everything on day 1. Consider a "knockouts
  on the last day" option. Low.

### 3.4 Score entry — the courtside loop

This is the screen organisers and editors use most under pressure.

- **No context in the dialog.** It says "Match #1" only. Show division, pool or round,
  court and time; with duplicate numbers (2.5) the title alone is ambiguous. Both teams get
  "SE" avatars here (Seed08/Seed09), so the initials add nothing. High.
- **The save loop is ~4.2s locally.** That is 2.4s to save plus a 1.8s refetch of the whole
  payload (`View.jsx:143`). Have the PUT return what changed, and update optimistically.
  High.
- **No match-format awareness.** It starts with one 0-0 set and you click "+ Add Set" each
  time. There is no check for a valid volleyball score (25/15, win by 2). "End Match" is
  allowed with level sets (1-1), which also triggers 4.1. Once a division has a match
  format: add sets automatically, warn on unusual scores, and block ending a knockout match
  without a winner. High.
- **"Cancel Match" is ambiguous.** In most UIs "Cancel" means close this dialog. Rename it
  "Mark as not played" or "Forfeit / walkover…" and move it into a "More" menu. Medium.
- **Edits can be lost.** × discards without a warning, and Escape does nothing. Low.
- **Tall on phones.** With the steppers stacked vertically, two sets fill the screen.
  Low.

### 3.5 Round progression

- **The banner sends you to the wrong division.** "A round is complete" doesn't say which
  division. "Go to Standings" opens the first division alphabetically — in the test,
  League 5, which had nothing to progress — rather than the one that is ready (Pools 12).
  Name the division(s) in the banner and deep-link to each. Medium.
- **The Next Round dialog hides pool positions.** It lists qualifiers as "Seed01 — W:2 L:0
  Sets 4-0" with no A1/B1. With identical records the organiser can't see why 1–4 are in
  that order. Show pool positions and the whole bracket path, not only the next round.
  Medium.
- **A progression can't be undone.** Known (re-progression guard unreachable).

### 3.6 Settings tab

- **Tournament details can't be edited.** There is no endpoint and no UI to change the
  name, dates, location or description after creation. A typo in the name is permanent.
  High.
- **The placement-depth select is truncated** ("4th (final and 3rd place onl"). Low.
- **Ranking options aren't explained** ("FIVB points", "Simplified points"). Add a line of
  help per option. Low.
- **Ranking basis can change after progression.** The pool table then re-sorts while the
  bracket stays as committed, so spectators see a table that contradicts who advanced. Lock
  it once the next round has started, or say what basis was used at progression. Medium.

### 3.7 Lifecycle and destructive actions

- **"End Tournament" misdescribes itself.** It says "No further results can be recorded",
  and the help text repeats this (`helpContent.js:92`). Verified: a result PUT after ending
  returns 200, and `View.jsx:196` deliberately keeps score entry open. Pick one behaviour.
  Allowing corrections is sensible; then reword it: "Marks the tournament finished.
  Results can still be corrected." High.
- **End doesn't warn about unfinished business.** It says nothing about unplayed fixtures or
  divisions that haven't reached their final, and there is no reopen. Medium.
- **Delete is one "Yes".** A whole tournament goes after a single Yes/No click. Use
  type-the-name-to-confirm, or a soft delete with a 7-day restore. Medium.
- **The confirm dialog is bare.** It is the same "Yes / No" for everything
  (`components/ConfirmDialog.jsx`): no title, no danger styling, no action-named buttons
  ("Delete tournament" / "Keep it"), no Escape, no focus trap, no `role="alertdialog"`.
  Medium.

### 3.8 Accounts (signup, login, profile, settings)

- **Missing account basics:**
  - Forgot password. Known.
  - Change email or username.
  - Delete account. The privacy policy promises deletion on request, so today it is a
    manual job.
  - Sign out of other devices.
  Medium.
- **Password rules appear only after you fail them,** as a 3-second toast. Show them
  inline, or better, drop the upper/lower/digit/symbol rule. Long passphrases are both
  stronger and easier. Add a show-password toggle. Medium.
- **Signup's success message is overwritten.** "Account created successfully" is replaced
  within the second by "Successfully logged in!" (verified), because Login's own
  `check-login` effect re-runs. Low.
- **No onboarding.** New users land on Home. Send them to "Create your first tournament"
  or back to where they came from. Low.
- **Profile has no "Tournaments I can edit".** Editors have no route to the tournaments
  they've been given. High for editors.

### 3.9 Cross-cutting UI

- **Toasts** (`MessageProvider.jsx:9`):
  - They have a fixed 3s life, which is too short for errors.
  - Each message starts a new timer and the old one isn't cleared, so a second message is
    dismissed early by the first message's timer.
  - There's no `aria-live`, so screen readers never hear them.
  - There's no close button.
  Medium.
- **Help button** (`App.jsx:93`):
  - It is a `<div onClick>`, so it can't be reached by keyboard and has no label.
  - It covers the footer's "Terms of Service" link.
  - Some help text is outdated (3.7).
  Medium.
- **Theme:**
  - The theme starts as `'light'` and switches in an effect (`ThemeContext.jsx:6`), so
    dark-mode users see a white flash on every load. Set the class before React mounts.
  - There's no "System" option.
  - The icon shows the current mode rather than the action.
  Low.
- **Login page:**
  - `autoComplete="password"` should be `current-password` (`Login.jsx:163`), which helps
    password managers.
  - The Login/Create toggle is a `<span onClick>` and the theme toggle an `<i onClick>`;
    neither is keyboard reachable.
  - × uses `navigate(-1)`, which leaves the site if /login was opened directly.
  - There's no logo or name on the page.
  Low.
- **Footer:**
  - `rel="noreferror"` typo (`App.jsx:348`).
  - "© 2025".
  - "Privacy Policy" and "Terms of Service" both go to the top of `/terms` rather than to
    `#privacy` and `#terms`.
  Low.
- **Manifest:**
  - The name is "Tournament Organiser", not the brand.
  - `favicon.ico` is marked `maskable`.
  - It declares `standalone` with no service worker, so the app installs but does nothing
    useful offline. Venue wifi is often poor.
  Low.
- **Browser support.** Relative colour syntax (`rgb(from var(--x) r g b / a)`) is
  unsupported before Safari 16.4 and Chrome 119. Older phones lose those backgrounds
  silently. Low.
- **Tap targets in the site chrome are under 44px.** Known.

## 4. Correctness bugs and algorithm issues

### 4.1 Cancelled or drawn knockout match breaks progression — Critical, verified live

`progression.service.js:323` and `:333`: a knockout group whose fixture is CANCELLED, or
COMPLETED with equal sets, pushes **no outcome**. The results list then shifts by one and
every index after it points at the wrong team.

Reproduced on the test tournament with quarterfinal 1 (Seed01 v Seed05) marked cancelled,
the others normal:

- The proposed semifinalists were **Seed02, Seed03, Seed04 and Seed06**. Seed06 *lost* its
  quarterfinal, and it was drawn against **Seed02, the team that had just beaten it**.
- Seed01 and Seed05 were dropped from `computedResults` entirely. Because `eligibleTeams` is
  `computed` (`:62`), the organiser cannot even swap them back in.

The client allows both triggers: "Cancel Match" and "End Match" with 1-1 sets.

**Fix direction:**

- A knockout fixture must not be completable without a winner (server and UI).
- "Cancel" in a knockout needs a winner: a walkover.
- Defensively, `buildKnockoutOutcomes` should refuse (409, naming the match) rather than
  shift silently.

### 4.2 Forfeits and withdrawals — High (Known as a feature idea)

The live HST data shows how organisers cope today:

- a team literally named **"BYE"** that "wins" 15-0, 15-0;
- forfeits typed in as 15-0/15-0, 0-0/15-15 or a single 0-15 set.

These distort point ratios, which decide tiebreaks, and interact with 4.1. Treat it as a
correctness issue, not a nice-to-have:

- a `FORFEIT` result with a chosen winner and a configurable nominal score;
- a "withdraw team" action that forfeits that team's remaining matches.

### 4.3 Same-pool teams can meet in the semifinals — Medium

With four pools and eight qualifiers, the quarterfinals are A1–D2, B1–C2, C1–B2, D1–A2. The
semifinals are `[0,3]` and `[1,2]`, so **A1 and A2 sit in the same half** (likewise B1/B2,
C1/C2, D1/D2) and can meet in a semifinal.

`tournament-rules.md` guarantees only that the *first* knockout round avoids same-pool
matches. Standard crossovers put each pool's 1st and 2nd in opposite halves:

- QF: A1–B2, C1–D2, B1–A2, D1–C2
- SF: winners of the first two, winners of the last two

That is also fairer than the current rule, where pool A's winner is always top seed purely
by pool letter.

### 4.4 Cross-pool comparisons with unequal pool sizes — Medium

`seedAcrossGroups` (`utils/standings.js:343`) compares the tier that straddles the
qualifying line using `primaryScore`: raw matches won, points or sets. With 10 teams in 3
pools, a team in the 4-team pool has played 3 matches and a team in a 3-team pool 2. Raw
totals favour the bigger pool.

Use per-match averages (or ratios only) when pools differ in size. Related to, but not the
same as, the Known gap about which pool fills uneven tiers.

### 4.5 Smaller correctness items

- **`updateResult` never checks tournament status.** Results are accepted before Start
  (the UI hides the button, the API doesn't care) and after End (3.7). Low.
- **No sanity check on scores.** The only rule is 0–999. A level set (25-25) is accepted and
  counts for nobody. Validate against the match format when that exists. Low.
- **`createTournament` trusts its input.** In `tournaments.service.js`:
  - dates aren't validated server-side (end before start, malformed → 500);
  - `divisions` missing → TypeError 500;
  - `divisions: []` → a tournament with zero divisions;
  - division and team name lengths aren't checked (Known);
  - `num_teams` is taken from the client rather than `teams.length`.
  Medium.
- **Dates come back in two formats.** `formatTournamentDates` (`tournaments.service.js:365`)
  sends `start_date` as "24 September 2026" and `end_date` as "2026-09-25" in the same
  object. The client copes, but it is a trap. Low.
- **Username and email aren't normalised** (`users.service.js:31`):
  - "Tom" and "tom" can both register.
  - Emails are case-sensitive, so "Foo@x.com" and "foo@x.com" are different accounts and
    login is case-sensitive.
  - A username may contain "@". Registering another user's email *as a username* makes
    `WHERE email=$1 OR username=$1` (`users.repository.js:34`) match two rows. The first
    wins, so the real owner can't log in by email.
  Lowercase emails, forbid "@" in usernames, and make usernames case-insensitively unique.
  Medium.

## 5. Backend performance and efficiency

### 5.1 The public list endpoint sends everything — Medium

`getAllTournaments` is `SELECT * FROM tournaments` (`tournament.repository.js:25`), with no
paging and no ordering. Every visitor to Discover downloads:

- every tournament's full `schedule` JSON (HST's alone is 31.7 KB of the 38 KB response
  with only 3 tournaments),
- `created_by` user ids,
- `scoresheet_template`,
- `last_update`.

This grows linearly with tournaments. Select only the card columns, and page or filter on
the server.

### 5.2 The tournament payload is large and duplicated — Medium

HST is **342 KB uncompressed**:

- `results` duplicates `fixtures` (21.8 KB × 6 divisions once complete).
- Dates appear twice (`start_date` and `startDate`).
- The raw `state` is included.
- The dashboard repeats each division's recent and upcoming lists.

The server has **no compression middleware**. Check whether Render compresses at its edge;
if not, add it. JSON compresses roughly 8–10×.

### 5.3 The 304 path does all the work anyway — Medium

`fetchTournamentDetails` loads every fixture and team and runs the whole formatter before
the controller compares ETags (`tournaments.controller.js:34-45`). The change key needs only
the tournament and division rows. Check `If-None-Match` first and return 304 before loading
fixtures. This is what makes live polling (2.3) nearly free.

### 5.4 Writes go row by row — Medium

- Creating a tournament inserts every team and every fixture with its own query
  (`divisions.service.js:678`). 17 teams and 30 fixtures took **10.5s**.
- Rebuild, reorder and progression binding do the same.

Use multi-row `INSERT … SELECT FROM unnest(...)`: one query per table per division.

### 5.5 N+1 and repeated reads — Low/Medium

- `getTeamsByDivision` (`tournaments.service.js:405`) runs one query per division,
  sequentially — 6 round-trips for HST. One `WHERE id = ANY($1)` over all ids does it.
- `updateResult` reads the division state `FOR UPDATE` up to three times, plus a fixtures
  query outside the transaction.

### 5.6 No indexes on foreign keys — Low now, Medium later

Postgres doesn't index FKs automatically. None of these are indexed:

- `fixtures.division_id`
- `divisions.tournament_id`
- `teams.division_id`
- `saved_tournaments.user_id`
- `tournaments.created_by`
- `tournament_editors.user_id`

Every tournament view and cascade delete will sequential-scan as data grows. This is a
schema change, so High Risk under CLAUDE.md.

### 5.7 Client retries can duplicate writes — Medium

`requests.js:47` retries any request that fails at the network level 5×, **POST included**.
If a create-tournament, add-division or progression request reaches the server and the
response is lost (a Render restart, a mobile network switch), the retry runs it again.
Retry only idempotent methods, or send an idempotency key.

### 5.8 Hosting region

Neon is in eu-central-1 (Frankfurt). If the Render service is not also in Frankfurt, every
query pays a transatlantic round-trip, and the per-row patterns above multiply it.
Co-locate them. Worth checking.

## 6. Security

### 6.1 CSRF — Critical if the production setup is as the code says (verify)

In production the session cookie is `SameSite=None` (`config/auth.js:16`), and the API
parses `application/x-www-form-urlencoded` bodies with `extended: true` (`app.js:61`). It
checks neither `Origin` nor a CSRF token.

A malicious page can auto-submit a hidden form to the API, and the browser will attach the
victim's cookie. Exposed:

- body-less POSTs: `/tournaments/:id/start`, `/end`, `/save`;
- nested urlencoded bodies, which `qs` turns into objects: create tournament, add division,
  commit progression.

Verified locally that a cross-origin form POST reaches the handler. It was only refused
(401) because the dev cookie is `Lax`.

**Fix:**

- Drop the urlencoded parser. The API is JSON-only.
- Reject mutating requests whose `Content-Type` isn't `application/json`.
- Check `Origin` against `FRONTEND_URL`.
- Use `SameSite=Lax` if the site and API can share a registrable domain (6.2).

### 6.2 Third-party cookie dependency — High if the domains differ (verify)

`SameSite=None` only matters if the frontend and API are on **different sites**, for example
`tourganiser.co.za` and `*.onrender.com`. In that setup the session is a third-party
cookie:

- Safari blocks those by default.
- Firefox partitions them.
- Chrome increasingly restricts them.

Login would then appear to work and silently fail on many phones. Serving the API as
`api.tourganiser.co.za`, or proxying `/api` through the frontend host, fixes this and 6.1
together.

### 6.3 No server-side size limits — High

Nothing on the server caps:

- teams per division;
- divisions per tournament;
- round-robin legs (the client caps at 10; `divisions.service.js:843` does not);
- schedule entries (Known).

One signed-in account can ask for a 1,000-team, 10-leg league: about 5 million fixtures,
inserted one row at a time on a pool connection. Add caps (e.g. 128 teams, 20 divisions, 5
legs) and reject over-large bodies.

### 6.4 Sessions and passwords — Medium

- **JWTs can't be revoked.** Logout only clears the cookie, and a password change doesn't
  end other sessions. A stolen cookie stays valid for 24h. Add a `token_version` on `users`
  and check it.
- **The password policy is client-side only.** The API accepts any non-empty password.
- **Login timing leaks.** An unknown user skips the bcrypt compare. Minor.
- **Email enumeration** is possible through signup and add-editor by email. Accepted in
  `decisions.md`; noted for completeness.
- The JWT carries a snapshot of username and email, so `/profile` goes stale if either ever
  becomes editable.

### 6.5 Minor

- `created_by` user ids are public via the list endpoint (5.1).
- The `admin` flag is shown on Profile and nowhere else — no moderation tools exist. Fine
  for now, but there is no way to take down an abusive public tournament.

## 9. Gaps and additions

Ordered by value for your current users: school and university volleyball tournaments, run
by one organiser plus helpers, followed by players and parents on phones.

### 9.1 High value

1. **Live spectator view.** Auto-refresh via ETag polling while visible, a "Live" badge, and
   "updated 20s ago".
2. **Team pages and "follow my team".** No login: remember it on the device. Shows next
   match, court and time; results; pool position; a share link. This is what most people at
   a tournament actually want.
3. **Match format per division.** Best of 3/5, set points (25/15), optional cap. It drives
   score validation, automatic set rows and auto-end, correct FIVB points, and scoresheets.
4. **Forfeit / walkover / withdraw team** as first-class actions (4.2).
5. **Edit tournament details** (3.6) and **visibility**: draft, unlisted (link only),
   public.
6. **Organiser setup checklist** (3.2).
7. **Sharing kit:**
   - copy-link and WhatsApp share buttons;
   - a QR code on the overview and on printed schedules ("scan for live results");
   - per-tournament titles and Open Graph previews (needs a tiny server-rendered meta
     endpoint, since this is an SPA).
8. **Courtside scorer mode.** For an editor: pick a court and see only that court's current
   and next match, with large +/- buttons. Optionally score point by point, which gives
   spectators live scores. Queue writes offline and sync when the wifi comes back.

### 9.2 Medium value

9. **Venue display / TV mode.** A full-screen board: now playing per court, next up, latest
   results, auto-rotating. Projectors in the hall are common at school tournaments.
10. **Running late.** "Shift all remaining matches by N minutes", and on one court only.
    Schedules always slip, and today every entry must be edited by hand.
11. **More formats:**
    - pool-aware crossovers (4.3);
    - an optional 3rd-place match;
    - single elimination (stubbed in code);
    - double elimination;
    - a second pool phase (gold/silver pools), common in volleyball;
    - Swiss.
12. **Duplicate tournament / templates.** HST 2027 = HST 2026 with new dates. Annual events
    are your core use.
13. **Import and export:**
    - import teams from CSV or a paste;
    - export results and standings (CSV/PDF);
    - a printable results summary.
14. **Editors done properly:**
    - "Tournaments I can edit" on Profile;
    - notify the person when added;
    - accept/decline;
    - per-court scorer roles.
15. **Result history / audit log.** `entered_by` keeps only the last writer. Keep every
    change ("21-19 changed to 21-17 by X at 10:42") — useful for disputes.
16. **Account completeness** (3.8) and **Neon dev branch** (7.4).

### 9.3 Later

17. Notifications: push or email ("your team plays in 15 min on Court 3").
18. Player rosters and per-player stats (Known), team logos and colours.
19. Admin moderation for public content.
20. Analytics — or remove the claim from the home page now (2.1).
21. Other sports, once volleyball is solid (Known direction).
